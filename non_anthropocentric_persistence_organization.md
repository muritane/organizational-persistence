# Boundary-Condition Organization and Non-Anthropocentric Persistence

## From Human Object Categories to Transfer-Regime Individuation

---

# Abstract

This document refines the persistence-organization framework by weakening the dependence on ordinary human object categories and replacing them with a more general account of individuation through boundary conditions, transfer functions, persistence mechanisms, and projection relations.

The central claim is:

> An organization, object, process, institution, planet, civilization, whirlpool, glass sphere, or visible pattern is distinct not because it is a sealed object, but because a relatively stable regime of relations, boundaries, transformations, and identity conditions makes it trackable across time and projections.

The aim is to recover distinctions that are easily obscured by human perceptual habits:

```text
solid object vs process
natural object vs social abstraction
physical thing vs informational pattern
closed boundary vs permeable interface
appearance vs reality
regeneration vs persistence
```

The framework does not treat human-visible objecthood as basic.

Instead, it treats distinctness as derived from:

```text
boundary conditions
transfer functions
internal relation stability
identity predicates
persistence mechanisms
support dependencies
projection stability
observer/sensor coupling
state-space separability
```

In compressed form:

> Objecthood is a projection. Persistence is a relation. Distinctness is a boundary-conditioned stability in state-space.

# 2. Distinctness Without Sealedness

A boundary is not necessarily a wall.

A boundary is a region, surface, interface, gradient, threshold, or transition across which relevant relations change.

A boundary may be:

```text
permeable
semi-permeable
reflective
refractive
absorptive
porous
diffuse
statistical
dynamical
functional
jurisdictional
informational
control-theoretic
identity-relevant
```

Therefore:

```text
boundary ≠ closure
boundary ≠ opacity
boundary ≠ isolation
boundary ≠ impermeability
```

A thing may be distinct while exchanging energy, matter, information, legitimacy, light, control, heat, labor, or memory with its environment.

In compressed form:

```text
distinctness requires differential transfer relations, not sealed enclosure
```

# 5. Glass Sphere Case

A glass sphere is distinct from air despite allowing light to pass through.

Its distinctness comes from boundary-conditioned transfer differences:

```text
material continuity
surface geometry
refractive index contrast
reflectance
refraction
partial absorption
thermal behavior
mechanical response
chemical composition
```

Light crossing the boundary is not evidence against distinctness.

It is evidence for distinctness because the crossing is transformed.

```text
incoming light
→ glass-air interface
→ reflected component + refracted component + transmitted component + distorted projection
```

The glass sphere is not distinct because it is opaque.

It is distinct because it maintains a stable interface and transformation regime.

Therefore:

```text
objecthood = stable boundary-conditioned transformation pattern
```

not:

```text
objecthood = sealed solid thing
```

# 8. Satellite Production and Participation

Earth can participate in satellite-producing processes, but this must be typed carefully.

There are different satellite cases:

```text
1. material production
2. relational capture
3. nested-organizational production
```

## 8.1 Moon-like Material Production

A Moon-like satellite may arise from high-energy planetary formation or impact processes:

```text
proto-Earth + impactor + angular momentum + debris dynamics
→ debris disk
→ accretion
→ Moon-like satellite
```

This is not a routine output of the present Earth-system.

It is a rare historical event in a planetary formation regime.

## 8.2 Temporary Natural Satellites

Earth may capture small bodies temporarily.

Here Earth does not materially produce the satellite.

It produces or participates in an orbital relation:

```text
asteroid + Earth gravity well + Sun/Moon perturbations
→ temporary Earth-bound trajectory
```

The output is not primarily a new object.

It is a state relation:

```text
external body becomes temporarily satellite-related to Earth
```

## 8.3 Artificial Satellites

Artificial satellites are produced by nested technological organizations within the Earth-system:

```text
Earth-system
  contains civilization
    contains industry
      contains launch systems
        produce artificial satellites
```

If civilization is included as a suborganization of Earth-system, then Earth-system can produce satellites through nested organizations.

If Earth is restricted to geophysical dynamics, artificial satellites are not direct Earth outputs.

Thus:

```text
Earth participates in satellite production under specific regimes.
Earth does not simply produce satellites as a routine geophysical output.
```

# 10. Organization Versus Agent

Not every organization is an agent.

An organization is a distinguishable persistence pattern with identity-relevant structure.

An agentic organization has control capacity directed toward steering future trajectories.

Thus:

```text
organization ≠ agent
```

A glass sphere is an organization in the weak sense:

```text
stable material/boundary organization
```

Earth is an organization in the planetary-system sense:

```text
coupled geophysical, atmospheric, oceanic, biological, orbital transformation regime
```

A civilization is an organization in the high-order composite sense:

```text
recursive support network with knowledge, infrastructure, coordination, and reproduction
```

A firm or robot may be an agentic organization:

```text
organization + observation + decision + actuation + goal-directed control
```

Control capacity should not be assumed for every organization.

It should be modeled when present.

# 11. Persistence Mechanisms

Persistence should not be identified with regeneration alone.

Persistence is identity-relevant continuity across time.

Regeneration is one mechanism of persistence.

Other mechanisms include:

```text
durability
maintenance
repair
replacement
migration
reproduction
external preservation
recognition
reconstruction
```

## 11.1 Durability

Durability is persistence through slow degradation relative to the relevant time horizon.

Example:

```text
diamond lattice persists because ordinary degradation is low
```

## 11.2 Regeneration

Regeneration is active restoration of identity-relevant structure.

Example:

```text
cell repairs membrane and maintains metabolism
```

## 11.3 Replacement

Replacement preserves identity while substituting components.

Example:

```text
ship replaces planks while maintaining recognized continuity
```

## 11.4 Migration

Migration preserves organization while changing substrate.

Example:

```text
archive moves from paper to digital storage
```

## 11.5 Recognition

Recognition preserves identity through external reference.

Example:

```text
legal institution persists through records, recognition, and procedural continuity
```

## 11.6 Reconstruction

Reconstruction re-instantiates a pattern from records, templates, or memory.

Example:

```text
software service restored from backup
```

Thus:

```text
persistence = broader category
regeneration = active persistence mechanism
```

# 12. State-Space Separability

An entity is distinguishable in state-space when variables internal to its tracking regime exhibit stronger mutual coupling, identity continuity, or transfer coherence than arbitrary environmental variables.

Let:

```text
X_O ⊂ X_W
```

represent a candidate organization state-space.

`O` is distinguishable when:

```text
coupling(X_O internal) > coupling(X_O with arbitrary exterior)
```

or when:

```text
boundary transfer τ_O is stable enough to support prediction, interaction, or identity tracking
```

This permits non-visual individuation.

Examples:

```text
glass sphere:
  material and optical coherence
```

```text
storm:
  rotating pressure/wind regime
```

```text
Earth:
  coupled planetary dynamics and boundary conditions
```

```text
language:
  distributed pattern of speakers, memory, norms, symbols, and reproduction
```

```text
civilization:
  recursively maintained support network with transmitted knowledge and coordinated material reproduction
```

# 16. Formal Sketch

Let:

```text
O = (X_O, B_O, τ_O, Id_O, M_O, Π)
```

where:

```text
X_O  = organization-relevant state variables
B_O  = boundary/interface regime
τ_O  = transfer function across boundary/interface
Id_O = identity predicate
M_O  = persistence mechanisms
Π    = set of projections
```

`O` is distinguishable when:

```text
B_O and τ_O remain stable enough to support Id_O over time
```

Persistence occurs when:

```text
∀t, Id_O(x_t, x_{t+1})
```

under at least one sufficient persistence mechanism:

```text
M_O ∈ {durability, maintenance, repair, regeneration, replacement, migration, reproduction, preservation, recognition, reconstruction}
```

A projection is:

```text
Π_i(O) = lossy representation of O under observer/sensor/model i
```

Object appearance is:

```text
ObjectView(O) = Π_visual/spatial(O)
```

but:

```text
ObjectView(O) ≠ O
```

# 17. Diagnostic Questions

For any candidate entity or organization, ask:

```text
1. What state variables are being tracked?
2. What boundary/interface regime distinguishes it?
3. What crosses the boundary?
4. How are crossings transformed?
5. What internal relations remain stable?
6. What identity predicate defines continuity?
7. Which persistence mechanisms preserve it?
8. Which supports are required?
9. Which projections make it visible or usable?
10. Which projections hide its support complexity?
11. Is it agentic, non-agentic, or nested inside agentic systems?
12. Is its boundary material, dynamical, informational, jurisdictional, optical, or mixed?
13. Is the apparent object a human-visual projection or a more general state-space distinction?
14. Where does the entity stop under this model?
15. What alternative boundary definitions are also valid?
```

The framework should not begin from the human perceptual category:

```text

```

```text
boundary-conditioned persistence pattern
```

The map should ask:

```text
Which boundary conditions exist?
Which transfer functions operate?
Which projections reveal or conceal the pattern?

Which support conditions preserve it?
```

In compressed form:

> A thing is distinct when a boundary-conditioned transfer regime preserves identity-relevant structure strongly enough to support tracking, interaction, projection, and persistence.

# 20. Closing Statement

This refinement moves the persistence framework away from human-centered object perception and toward a more general ontology of boundary-conditioned organization.

A glass sphere, Earth, whirlpool, institution, language, civilization, and robot are not separated by the crude opposition between physical object and abstraction.

They differ by:

```text
boundary type
transfer function
state-space separability
identity predicate
persistence mechanism
support dependency
projection regime
control capacity
viability structure
```

The goal is not to deny ordinary objecthood.

The goal is to derive objecthood from deeper relations.

In compressed form:

> Objecthood is a projection of stable boundary-conditioned organization. Persistence is identity-relevant continuity under degradation and transformation. A non-anthropocentric map edits the conditions and transfer regimes from which objects, processes, institutions, planets, civilizations, and visible worlds appear.
